I built Ammo = Life, a small first-person shooter in which ammunition also serves as health. Firing a shot reduces your health, and collecting ammunition restores it.

## Using one resource for health and ammo

I wanted to see how combining the two would affect the player's decisions. Each shot now carried a cost beyond using up ammunition: it also made the player easier to kill.

During playtesting, this made me more careful about firing. I had to weigh the risk of approaching an enemy against the health I would spend shooting from further away.

## What changed in the design

- Ammo placement affected both recovery and the pace of combat.
- Accuracy mattered more because missed shots also reduced health.
- Players had to balance conserving ammunition against clearing enemies from their path.

The demo was rough and was never intended as a full release. It helped me explore how one mechanic could affect several parts of a game without adding more systems.
