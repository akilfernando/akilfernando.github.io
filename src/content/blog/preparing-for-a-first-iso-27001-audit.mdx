Preparing for our first ISO 27001 audit taught me how much depends on keeping records while the work happens. The auditor needs to check that the Information Security Management System (ISMS) operates as documented, and we need evidence to demonstrate that.

## Collect evidence from the start

I initially treated documentation as something to finish after the controls were in place. That left me reconstructing records close to the audit.

I would start earlier with access review records, offboarding tickets, risk treatment decisions, management review minutes, and corrective actions. Keeping these as part of the work is easier than gathering them later.

## Know the main documents

The documents I needed to know best were:

- The scope statement, which defines what the ISMS covers and excludes.
- The Statement of Applicability, which records which Annex A controls apply and why.
- The risk assessment and treatment plan, which connects identified risks to the actions taken.
- Current, approved policies and the records showing how they are followed.

## Practice explaining the connections

I practiced choosing a control and tracing it from the risk assessment to the policy, configuration, and evidence. I also worked backwards from settings in the tenant to their justification.

This helped me find places where I could not clearly explain the process or locate the supporting record.

## Be clear about gaps

Known weaknesses should be recorded and addressed. I would rather explain a gap and its corrective action than describe a process as complete when the evidence does not support that.

## On the day

Keep the evidence organized and accessible. Involve the people who run the controls, since the auditor may want to speak with them directly. Answer the question asked and use the records to support the answer.
