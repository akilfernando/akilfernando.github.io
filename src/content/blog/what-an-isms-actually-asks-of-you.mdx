When I joined Teams Squared as IT Operations & Cybersecurity Lead, the company had completed Stage 1 of its ISO 27001 audit, which reviewed the management system documentation. Many of the documented processes still needed to be put into practice.

As the operational lead for the Information Security Management System (ISMS), I needed to understand the existing documents, revise them where they did not fit our operations, and help the team implement the processes they described.

## Security for a remote team

Our staff and contractors work from home across several regions. We use Microsoft Entra ID for identity and access management, NinjaOne for device management and patching, and Bitdefender GravityZone for endpoint protection.

The internal team was roughly a dozen people at the time, with many recent joiners. The procedures needed to be practical enough for that team to follow alongside its other work.

## Preparing for Stage 2

Stage 2 checks whether the management system operates as documented. Preparing for it meant reviewing nearly all of our ISO 27001 documents. I wrote several new procedures and revised dozens of existing ones to reflect the work we were doing and the changes we needed to make.

Much of the implementation was my responsibility. Our ISMS Owner and COO, Amresh Selvaskandan, was actively involved, which helped us coordinate the changes across the company.

At the time of writing, our Stage 2 audit was scheduled for 31 August. I wanted us to be able to show both that the controls were operating and that we had reliable records of them.

## Keeping the system running

The work continues after the audit. ISO 27001 requires continual improvement under Clause 10, including identifying nonconformities and taking corrective action.

For us, that means reviewing risks, monitoring controls, recording problems, and checking whether the changes we make address them. Those tasks need to remain part of routine operations as the company grows.
